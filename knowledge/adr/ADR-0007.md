# ADR-0007 - Scope identity: single-tenant local GitOps rehearsal; the multi-customer IDP is a separate future project

Settles the two "decisions in flight" recorded in `knowledge/activeContext.md`
(2026-07-19 ecosystem review, finding R6): what this repository is, and what to do
about its stale name.
Proposed by an agent and accepted by the human maintainer on 2026-07-22;
`adr_status: accepted` records that decision.

## Context

The repository's own constitution already describes a narrow product: a
deterministic, high-speed ephemeral Kubernetes environment for a single developer on
a single Ubuntu workstation, provisioning one local Talos cluster and reconciling a
turnkey GitOps payload (`knowledge/constitution.md` "Why this project exists").
Its non-goals are equally explicit: multi-user / access-restricted identity is out of
scope, CAPI and a management cluster are deferred, and every accepted security gap is
justified as "single-user local dev tool"
(`knowledge/constitution.md` "Non-goals"; `clusters/workload/README.md` "Known
limitations").

Despite that, a larger ambition - a multi-customer ephemeral IDP - has lived
implicitly in the project's framing (the "IDP" in its title, the original
multi-cluster repository layout).
The 2026-07-19 ecosystem review (finding R6) called this out: treating this repo as
"the IDP" overstates its scope, and the mismatch between constitution and framing
leaves future work unanchored.

Separately, the repository's name is a stale artifact: `kind` was removed entirely by
ADR-0001 (the CAPD/kind management-cluster design was dropped for direct `talosctl`
provisioning), so `kind-talos-gitops` names a component the repo no longer contains.

## Decision

1. **This repository's identity is the single-tenant, single-cluster local GitOps
   rehearsal tool its constitution describes.**
   Its job is to prove, locally and reproducibly: Talos provisioning
   (`talosctl cluster create`, Docker provisioner), the in-cluster Flux loop, the
   layered `controllers`/`configs` Kustomization pattern, and the turnkey payload
   (Cilium, cert-manager + local Root CA, Dex, Cloudflare Tunnel) - with SOPS/AGE
   secrets and the CAPI-consumability contract intact.
2. **The multi-customer ephemeral IDP becomes a separate future project with its own
   PRD.**
   It consumes this repository's verified patterns (Talos provisioning approach, Flux
   loop shape, layered Kustomizations, turnkey payload composition) as inputs, rather
   than growing inside this repo's framing.
   Multi-tenancy, access-restricted identity, and customer separation are that
   project's concerns from day one; none of them retrofit cleanly onto a
   single-tenant rehearsal tool, and this repo stops carrying them as implicit
   ambitions.

### Recorded option: renaming the repository

The name question is recorded here as an option, not executed:

- **Rename now.** Rejected for now: the rename's blast radius is the Flux
  `GitRepository` URL (`url: ssh://git@github.com/x45dev/kind-talos-gitops.git` in
  `clusters/workload/flux-system/gotk-sync.yaml`); renaming breaks Flux source
  reconciliation on any running cluster until the URL is updated and re-applied, and
  the deploy key binding moves with the repo. No functional gain justifies that
  churn on its own.
- **Rename with the next URL-touching change** (recommended): when a deliberate
  change already touches the `GitRepository` URL or requires re-bootstrapping the
  Flux source, fold the rename in; the migration cost is then marginal.
- **Never rename.** Rejected: the name misdescribes the repo (no `kind` anywhere in
  it, per ADR-0001) and will keep confusing future readers and tooling.

Recommendation: **defer the rename until a natural URL-touching change**, then
execute it deliberately (GitHub redirects old clone URLs, but the in-cluster
`GitRepository` object does not follow redirects for SSH URLs and must be repointed
explicitly).

## Alternatives considered

1. **Grow the multi-customer IDP inside this repository.**
   Rejected: it contradicts the constitution's invariants and non-goals (single-user,
   single-workstation, accepted single-tenant security gaps), and every hardening
   shortcut this repo legitimately took would become a latent multi-tenant
   vulnerability rather than an accepted local-dev trade-off.
2. **Rewrite the constitution to the larger ambition instead.**
   Rejected: Phases 0-3 were built and verified against the narrow scope; widening
   the constitution after the fact would retroactively misdescribe what was actually
   validated and would put the promotion evidence for ADR-0001..0006 on false
   footing.
3. **Leave the scope implicit (status quo).**
   Rejected: the ecosystem review showed the ambiguity actively misleads (this repo
   read as "the IDP"), and the constitution's non-goals were doing silent load-bearing
   work against that reading.

## Consequences

- Future feature specs in this repo (starting with `docs/specs/002-*`, Phase 4
  hardening) are scoped against the rehearsal-tool identity; multi-tenant features
  are out of scope here by decision, not just by omission.
- The future IDP project starts from its own PRD and inherits verified patterns by
  reference to this repo, with its own threat model; this repo's accepted gaps
  (Dex without org restriction, unrestricted `flux-system` egress, `noTLSVerify`
  tunnel hop) are explicitly non-transferable defaults.
- The repository keeps its current name until a deliberate URL-touching change; the
  rename option and its blast radius are recorded above so the eventual rename is an
  execution step, not a re-litigation.
- `knowledge/activeContext.md`'s two "decisions in flight" resolve to this ADR:
  scope identity decided here (accepted 2026-07-22), rename recorded-deferred.
